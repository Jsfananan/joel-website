Why most AI rollouts fail

Everyone's chasing the shiny object: the new tool, the AI agent, the slick workflow. Then they bolt it onto a structure that was never built for it, run by people who aren't ready for it, and a real investment fails. MIT found that about 95% of enterprise AI pilots delivered no measurable business impact. It's almost never the tool. It's the foundation underneath it.

Some of your team is skeptical. Some are eager but have no on-ramp. Some are hands-on but don't trust it. A few are already ahead of everyone else. Each one needs a different thing from you, and most rollouts hand them all the same one: a tool, a memo, a training, a deadline.

When electricity first reached a town, no home got better overnight. You had to wire the house first. AI is the same. The tool is the easy part. The judgment underneath it is the wiring.

The foundation is two things

A tool only works when the structure around it is built for it, and the people using it are ready for it. In the workshop, you build both.

Part one · the structure

What to hand off, keep, and protect

The three decisions that wire the house: what you hand to AI, what you keep in human hands, and what you never let it touch. You leave with these written down for your real work, as a 90-day plan.

Part two · the people

The four kinds of people in every rollout

The all-in one. The eager one who hasn't started. The one who's unsure. The one using it but not trusting it. You'll learn to see which is which, and how to meet each one where they are, so your people are ready for the tool instead of run over by it.
